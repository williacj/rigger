ABOUTME: Records card #368, which keeps a kill from dropping survivors unrecorded when one of its
reads of the process table fails, and what was learned about how `ps` reports a failed read.

# 2026-09-28 — A kill records every process it ends, whatever its reads do

**A survivor a read leaves out was taken as gone.** The kill's rounds dropped a pending survivor
from the kill as soon as one read of the table did not list it, and recorded it only where L0 had
sent it the kill. So a read that listed nothing, and `run()` hands back nothing for `ps` exiting 1
with nothing printed, emptied every survivor still to be killed at once, and the group's later
kill ended them unrecorded. A `ps` stand-in failing that way once, on the kill's second read of a
chain 3 deep, reproduced it every time: one of four processes recorded. Reads that exit 0 with
part of the table, or with nothing on standard output and a failure on standard error, did the
same.

**Signal 0 decides whether a survivor is gone.** A survivor a read leaves out, and L0 has not sent
the kill, now stays to be read again while signal 0 still reaches it. A read that failed once
costs a round; one that keeps leaving a live survivor out holds the kill to its deadline, where the
group is recorded unnamed. A pid the system hands on to another process in between costs the same,
which records the group rather than a false name.

**The census had the same gap.** Codex and the engineer on #369 showed that a census whose group
reads all exit 1 silently agrees on an empty group, so the survivor was killed and nothing, not
even the group's kill, was recorded. The census now keeps an empty answer only where signal 0 no
longer reaches the group; otherwise it reads again, and at its deadline fails saying why, so the
group is recorded as killed. A group of zombies alone is unaffected: its reads list the zombies.

**A census can also name too few.** Codex's round 2 on #369 showed a census whose reads agreed on
one of two live survivors: the kill then ended both and recorded one. The kill now fails on any
live member its reads find that the census did not name, so the group is recorded as killed, and
it reads the table at least once even where the census named no one, which catches a census that
saw only a zombie beside a live survivor.

**A process every read leaves out is ended by the group's kill.** Codex's round 3 on #369 left a
live survivor out of every read of the census and of the kill: the kill named and ended the other,
and the group's kill in `ended()` then ended this one unrecorded. Before that kill the group is now
read once more, and where signal 0 still reaches it and the read finds a member that is not a
zombie, or fails, the group's kill is recorded beside the names. That read is the last chance the
table has: a process that joins the group after it is ended unrecorded.

**The start-time read left standard error to its parent.** #365's `startOf` read the leader's
start time through `execFileSync`, which passes standard error through, so a read that printed a
start time and a failure counted as good. It now reads through `spawnSync` and fails on any
standard error, as `run` does.

**`ps` writes every failure to standard error.** Measured with `ps` from adv_cmds-240 on macOS 27.0
(26A428): each of the adapter's five forms of read, given nothing to match, exits 1 and writes
nothing anywhere; a bad group or keyword exits 1 with a message. The source of adv_cmds-237, the
latest Apple publishes, exits 1 silently only once it kept no process, and exits 0 with nothing on
standard output where its read of the kernel's table fails, writing why to standard error. So
`run()` now takes a read that wrote anything to standard error as failed, whatever its exit code.
What `ps` cannot show is that the kernel handed it every process, which is why the kill still asks
signal 0.

**The engineer's hypothesis is not established.** Fifty chains run through main at `83a5dc5`,
instrumented to record every kill read's exit code, row count and standard error and every
survivor dropped unrecorded, never dropped one: 15 at 1,500 deep and 15 at 2,000 were killed
unnamed at the deadline, and at 1,000 deep 4 of 20 were named in full and 16 killed unnamed. No
read of any of them exited 1, or wrote to standard error, while a survivor was pending. So the
443-of-1,501 and 281-of-2,001 runs were not reproduced here. The hypothesis fits their shape, a
round emptying every pending survivor at once, and so does a read exiting 0 with nothing on
standard output, which `ps`'s source allows; both are now handled, but which, if either, it was is
not known.
