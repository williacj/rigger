# #486 — the maker dispatched through L1, and what the verbs say of it

**A harness that answers L2's role from no declared role is missing a grant that nobody saw.**
The first grant table read every line that broke when the verbs dispatched a maker. It missed two
harnesses, `attemptWorld`'s `l2Answer` and `pull-outcomes`' `decide`, which call `nextAction`
with no `roles`. Their tests went on passing, because L2's maker answer then names no provider and
`roleDispatch` rejects with `NOT_STARTED` before any process starts. But the granted rows saying
"the stand-in ran" could never hold. The exploratory run found the reddening lines (#486's
`facts-pull:69` and `restart-kill:292`). Only reading the harnesses found the silent ones. O78
granted all four.

**A restart's own maker dispatch looks, to an old reader, like the dead engine's.** In
`restart-kill`, `sweepSkipped` took the stream's first `dispatch.start` as the dead engine's. In
three of its tests the test wrote the record entry itself, so before this card the restart held
the stream's only dispatches, and it held none of them. Once the restart dispatches a maker, that
first start is its own. The reader now skips the restart's run, which wrote the stream's last
event.

**The verb tells a card left in Coding from a halt by a code, not by its message.** "Its maker
exited 0 and opened no pull request" has to be the card's one line, with no second line for L2's
failure, while an event the record refused must still be said whole. So L2's withheld failure
now carries `REVIEW_WITHHELD` and the facts it read (`src/workflow/transitions.mjs`, outside the
card's "Lands in"). L3 hands the maker's outcome and L2's settle on unread, as
`{ card, workspace, outcome, settled }`.

**A test whose fake forge answers from memory cannot tell an awaited settle from an unawaited
one.** With the forge read answering in the same turn, a pull that never awaited L2's settle
still showed card 2 in Review by the time the test looked. The mutation that dropped the await
passed. The maker world's forge read now answers one turn of the event loop later, as a real read
would, and that mutation fails the test.

**A wait on a directory change, armed after the change it waits for may come, can sleep for
ever.** The stand-in wrote its `held` file and only then armed `fs.watch` for its release. On macOS
a watch starts delivering a moment after it is armed. A release written in that moment, after the
stand-in's own check of the file, raised no event the watch saw, so the stand-in held for ever.
The engineer's probe on #556 met this in 2 of 40 runs at a load of 23. The gate's timeout at
`maker-dispatch.test.mjs:279` was this. A release is now `SIGUSR1`, and the stand-in adds its
listener before it writes `held`. A signal sent after the file appears cannot be missed, whatever
the timing. `test/stand-in-agent.test.mjs` releases forty runs, each in the turn its hold is seen.
At the old wait it failed on its assertion in 1 of 12 runs, with four copies at once; it does not
force the moment every time.

**A refusal "while B runs" has to be arranged, not hoped for.** The first two-card tests took for
granted that card 1's maker event came while card 2's maker ran. The engineer measured that it
never did: card 1 reached its maker first every time. Card 1 now waits at a step, a `read` on a
FIFO, until card 2's stand-in holds. The test asserts what the refusal saw of card 2, and it
releases card 2 only once card 1's slot is released. A refusal that killed every recorded group
now fails the test on an assertion, not a timeout.
