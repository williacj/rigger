# #539 — never stop a group

**The first route was wrong, and measured so.** The card first asked L0 to resume a child that
detached while the census held its group stopped, found by its parent. #528's probe against that
route still left a `git maintenance` stopped under pid 1: git's `daemonize` exits the parent as it
forks, so the child is under pid 1 before it calls `setsid`, and in one call the census's first
read of the group listed no process at all. Nothing in the process table ties such a child to the
group it left. The owner chose option B (O62): never stop the group.

**The probe, at a starting load of 38 or more.** The load was raised with 20, then 40, bounded
`perl` spinners (`alarm 2400`). At the base, `074480a`, 28 runs of 300 `git fetch`es each, at
starting loads from 38.02 to 188.49, recorded 32 census kills of a maintenance process still in
its group, and one run, started at 87.60, left two maintenance processes `Ts` under pid 1, each in a
group of its own and working in the probe's clone; those calls recorded `output.held` for groups
35159 and 35179. At the head, 22 runs of 300, started at loads from 60.97 to 125.82, left none.
In 12 of those calls the group was still occupied at the kill, and each kill loop ended in its
first round. That the race was rarer than when #528 measured it, because spinners load the
processor and not the process table as the suites then did, is a judgment, not a measurement.

**Retargets, and the stand-ins that keyed on the old kill.** Thirteen tests outside the first grant
failed under B because their `ps` stand-ins waited on the kill's per-round `ppid=` reads, or on a
census that never found the group stopped (O76). Each now keys on the read of the group's states
just before the kill, or forces the census's reads to disagree with a `?` state. A test of the `?`
re-read only discriminates where the round it rejects would record something different, so its
stand-in also answers the first name read with `mid-exec`.

**Two defects found while drafting, both fixed before the first commit.** A read just before the
kill that left out a member the census named, while signal 0 still reached it, took that member as
exited, so the kill would end it unrecorded; a member left out now counts as gone only once signal 0
no longer reaches it. And with a read timeout near `TIMER_MAX`, the extra margin a read is given
pushed Node's timeout past `TIMER_MAX`, which Node runs out at once; every read now caps it there.

**The directory census killed an unkillable process again on every round.** Its loop lists, stops
and kills until a list holds nothing it may signal. A process that outlives the kill was listed,
stopped and killed again until the read timeout. It now kills each pid once, waits up to
`KILL_BOUND` for it to end, then resumes it, since it stopped it, and records it as not ended.
