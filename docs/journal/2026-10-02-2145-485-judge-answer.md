# #485 — L2's judge answer

**The judge answer is its own L2 module, and the findings format lives in it.** `src/workflow/judges.mjs`
holds `judgeAnswer` and the one constant `FINDINGS`. The instruction fills it in, and the comment
reader builds its pattern from it, so ruling 2 AQ2's "one constant in one L2 module" is a single
module and not two. `nextAction` does not reach it yet. #488 makes that switch, in the same change
that teaches L3 to dispatch a judge.

**The facts are read outcomes, so #488 can hand `Promise.allSettled` straight through.** The judge
answer reads four more fields on the card's `forge` facts: `pull` (`{ number, base, head }`, with
both as SHAs), `diff`, `comments` and `editedAt`. Each is a read's settled outcome. The repository
reads already exist (`readMergeBase`, `readDiff`, `readComments`, `readEditedAt`). A rejected
`pull`, `comments` or `editedAt` read throws, naming the card and the read (reviewer 12). A rejected
`diff` is taken to be the forge declining to serve it: it is recorded as `diff.refused` and answered
`ignore` (Codex 5). The card does not split a refused diff from a diff read that failed some other
way, so nothing here splits them either.

**A judge's withheld step is told once by L3, because L2 keeps no memory.** `outcomes` holds each
judge's step outcomes, keyed by role. A judge whose required step failed is left out of the answer,
and `judge.withheld` is recorded naming the role, the step and the environment class. L2 records
that each time it is handed the failure, so #488's L3 should hand a judge's outcomes once, when its
steps end. Every other judge is answered exactly as it would be with no outcomes at all, and no
`again` is answered, so nothing retries within the claim (ruling 2 P3).

**Choices the acceptance left open, made here.**

- The earlier head is the newest head, other than the current one, named in a findings comment by
  one of the kind's judges. A comment that names a role the kind does not list counts for nothing,
  the earlier head included.
- The tier refusal checks every agent judge of the kind, whether or not it already has findings,
  because the labels are wrong either way. It names every conflict, together with the form check's
  reason, in one refusal, as `nextAction` does for the maker.
- A body never edited has `editedAt` null. The facts' `revision` is null, and the evidence says so.
