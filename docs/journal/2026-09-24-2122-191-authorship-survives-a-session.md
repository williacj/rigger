ABOUTME: Why card #191 proposes role authorship across sessions and separates the author from a card's filer.

# Card #191: the session ended, but the acceptance still has an author

`R-CARD-3` calls the decomposing role the author of a card it produces. `R-CARD-5` then permits
only that author to revise the acceptance. Neither row says whether the role's next session
inherits the first session's authority. Card #191 names the concrete failure: a judge can return
an insufficient acceptance under `R-LOOP-6` after the writing session has ended, with no one
identified to receive it.

The proposed D20 keeps authorship with the owner or authoring role across sessions. A different
filer remains a filer. The event record distinguishes that durable author from the session that
acted, making the two existing observations in `R-CARD-3` and `R-CARD-6` usable when a role
drafts and another session files.

The alternative of assigning authorship to the filer would make filing a transfer of the power
to revise. That would contradict `R-CARD-3` for a PM-produced card filed by another session. The
alternative of binding authorship to the original session would leave the return in
`R-LOOP-6` without an available actor whenever that session ended.

This is a proposal, not a ratified rule. Its cost is an extra handoff when a filer sees a weak
acceptance. The owner must decide whether that cost is preferable to transferring authorship at
filing. No requirement row is proposed; D20 chooses how existing `R-CARD-3`, `R-CARD-5`, and
`R-CARD-6` work together.
