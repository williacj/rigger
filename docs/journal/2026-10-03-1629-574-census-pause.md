ABOUTME: Journal for #574: why the census CPU-ratio test at process-adapter.test.mjs:942 failed on CI,
how the cause was forced, and the change to the census's pause that removes it.

# #574 — the census's pause, per read

**The cause.** Each census round makes five reads in this test: two of states, two of command
lines and one name. From #553 (merged at `27be550`) on, every round makes all five, where before it
stopped at the first read of states that was not all stopped. The census still paused once per
round, up to `LONGEST_PAUSE`, 50 ms. So its re-reads cost five reads' processor time per pause, not
one. On CI the failed run, 37152319112 attempt 1, check (20), made 86 reads in 1,475 ms and used
150.431 ms.

**The evidence is the test's own diagnostic.** It was harvested from the last 60 CI runs on 2026-10-03,
61 attempts in all, from each check job's log through `gh api`. Node 20 jobs on branches
without #553 made 28 to 32 reads and used 25.5 to 68 ms in all but one run. The one outlier used
133.5 ms in 28 reads. Node 20 jobs on branches with #553 made 83 to 121 reads and used 75 to
150.4 ms. The share of the bound rose from a median of about 0.3 to about 0.8.

**Where the time went in one run.** A trace of every read and pause, made locally at the base and
never committed, put about 85% of the call's processor time inside the reads of the re-read
window. With the forcing applied, at a starting load of 14.09, that was 113 of 184 ms.

**The forcing.** It was applied to the test, never committed. It makes the stand-in pad each state
it prints with 2 MiB of spaces, which raises what each read costs the caller. This host's cost per
read then passes CI's. At the base, 10 runs out of 10 failed, using 187 to 228 ms over about
1,480 ms. At the head, 10 out of 10 passed, using 65 to 77 ms. Every run started at a one-minute
load from 15.67 to 23.45. Adding survivors did not force the cause. With 40 survivors, each read of
the group took longer to run too, so the share stayed under the bound, at 0.77.

**The change.** Each read of a round now earns the pause one look earns, doubling from 1 ms to
`LONGEST_PAUSE`. The census waits out a round's pauses together after the round, never past its
deadline. A census of one live process now pauses 31 ms after its first round, where it paused
1 ms before. Its re-reads cost one read per 50 ms in the steady state, as they did before #553.

**The new test** compares when rounds begin, not processor time, so load does not decide it. At
the base's census, each round began 60 to 115 ms after the one before. At the head, each round
from the third on began at least 250 ms after the one before, for its five reads.
