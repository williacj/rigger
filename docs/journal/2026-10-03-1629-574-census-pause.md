ABOUTME: Journal for #574: why the census CPU-ratio test at process-adapter.test.mjs:942 failed on CI,
how the cause was forced, and the change to the census's pause that removes it.

# #574 — the census's pause, per read

**The cause.** Each census round makes five reads in this test: two of states, two of command
lines and one name. From #553 (merged at `27be550`) on, every round makes all five, where before it
stopped at the first read of states that was not all stopped. The census still paused once per
round, up to `LONGEST_PAUSE`, 50 ms. So its re-reads cost five reads' processor time per pause, not
one. On CI the failed run, 37152319112 attempt 1, check (20), made 86 reads in 1,475 ms and used
150.431 ms.

**The evidence is the test's own diagnostic.** It was harvested from the last 60 CI runs on 2026-10-03,
61 attempts in all, from each check job's log through `gh api`. Node 20 jobs on branches
without #553 made 28 to 32 reads and used 25.5 to 68 ms in all but one run. The one outlier used
133.5 ms in 28 reads. Node 20 jobs on branches with #553 made 83 to 121 reads and used 75 to
150.4 ms. The share of the bound rose from a median of about 0.3 to about 0.8.

**Where the time went in one run.** A trace of every read and pause, made locally at the base and
never committed, put most of the call's processor time inside the reads of the re-read window:
71 of 88 ms (81%) without the forcing, at a starting load of 47.93, and 113 of 184 ms (61%) with
it, at 14.09, where the forcing's 2 MiB strings add garbage collection between reads.

**The forcing.** It was applied to the test, never committed. It makes the stand-in pad each state
it prints with 2 MiB of spaces, which raises what each read costs the caller. This host's cost per
read then passes CI's. At the base, 10 runs out of 10 failed, using 187 to 228 ms over about
1,480 ms. At the head, 10 out of 10 passed, using 65 to 77 ms. Every run started at a one-minute
load from 15.67 to 23.45. Adding survivors did not force the cause. With 40 survivors, each read of
the group took longer to run too, so the share stayed under the bound, at 0.77.

**The change.** Each read of a round now earns the pause one look earns, doubling from 1 ms to
`LONGEST_PAUSE`. The census waits out a round's pauses together after the round. A census of one
live process now pauses 31 ms after its first round, where it paused 1 ms before. Its re-reads cost
one read per 50 ms in the steady state, as they did before #553.

**The cap.** The engineer judging the first head showed that the earned pause grows with the group:
a round holds four reads and one per live member, so at about 95 members one disagreeing round
earned the whole 5,000 ms `READ_TIMEOUT`, and the census kept that round without reading again. At
the base it read again 1 ms later. The card's author added an item for it. Now, where the time left
would hold another round as long as the last one took, the census reads again, its pause cut to at
most half of what the time left holds beyond one such round. Where it would not, the census waits
out its pause, never past its deadline, and keeps the last round, as the base did once a read ran
out of time.

**Why half, and not twice the last round.** The cap first left twice the last round's length. On
macOS CI at `997947f`, pull-request run 37223938436's check (20), the 100-survivor test failed:
the census made three reads of states, not four, so its second round did not finish before the
deadline. Its diagnostic gave 2 rounds and 106 reads over 3,930 ms. A second round can run more than twice as long as the first when the host's
load moves between them, and `node --test` runs test files side by side. Locally at a load near
22, the first round took 706 to 891 ms and the second began with 1,419 to 1,791 ms left, close to
the edge. Cut to half of what remains beyond one round, the second round begins with about 2,500 ms
left. The cost is a few more rounds just before the deadline of a census that never agrees: the
`:942` test made about 51 reads, where it made 36, and stayed at about two thirds of its bound
under the forcing.

**The new tests.** The first times the pause itself, from the end of a round's last read to the
start of the next round's first, on a compiled stand-in's clock. The first version timed the gap
between round starts, which held five reads' run, and Codex's first round showed that a loaded base
could pass it. What the new measure still holds besides the pause is one turn of Node's event loop
and the start of the next read's process. The base fails it unless that comes to about 200 ms on
every pause it times. The second test kills 100 survivors whose first read of states shows each
caught mid-exec. It requires a second round that agrees, within the deadline, and that begins with
at least half of what the first round left. With the cap removed, the census made one round and
then waited out its deadline.

**This Linux cloud container.** Its PID 1, `process_api`, never reaps orphans, so a killed survivor
stays a zombie, and the reads after the kill poll until `UNREAPED_BOUND`. The `:942` test counts
those reads' processor time too: at `2dbc5e2`, unforced, it took 58 reads and 95.7 ms here, where
macOS CI took 36 reads. Under a subreaper that reaps at once, as macOS's launchd does, it took 30
to 42 reads. The 2 MiB pad also costs the caller about 5 ms a read here, more than a tenth of a
50 ms pause, so it failed the head too. A 512 KiB pad sits between the base's cost and the head's on
this host. Both were found in exploratory runs at `2dbc5e2`, where the base failed at 182 and 185
ms and the head passed at 102 to 113 ms. The counted runs are in #577.
