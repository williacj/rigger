ABOUTME: Journal for #574: why the census CPU-ratio test at process-adapter.test.mjs:942 failed on CI,
how the cause was forced, and the change to the census's rounds that removes it.

# #574 — the census's rounds that cannot agree

**The cause.** Each census round makes five reads in this test: two of states, two of command
lines and one name. From #553 (merged at `27be550`) on, every round makes all five, where before it
stopped at the first read of states that was not all stopped. The census still paused once per
round, up to `LONGEST_PAUSE`, 50 ms. So its re-reads cost five reads' processor time per pause, not
one. On CI the failed run, 37152319112 attempt 1, check (20), made 86 reads in 1,475 ms and used
150.431 ms.

**The evidence is the test's own diagnostic.** It was harvested from the last 60 CI runs on 2026-10-03,
61 attempts in all, from each check job's log through `gh api`. Node 20 jobs on branches
without #553 made 28 to 32 reads and used 25.5 to 68 ms in all but one run. The one outlier used
133.5 ms in 28 reads. Node 20 jobs on branches with #553 made 83 to 121 reads and used 75 to
150.4 ms. The share of the bound rose from a median of about 0.3 to about 0.8.

**Where the time went in one run.** A trace of every read and pause, made locally at the base and
never committed, put most of the call's processor time inside the reads of the re-read window:
71 of 88 ms (81%) without the forcing, at a starting load of 47.93, and 113 of 184 ms (61%) with
it, at 14.09, where the forcing's 2 MiB strings add garbage collection between reads.

**The forcing.** It was applied to the test, never committed. It makes the stand-in pad each state
it prints with 2 MiB of spaces, which raises what each read costs the caller. This host's cost per
read then passes CI's. At the base, 10 runs out of 10 failed, using 187 to 228 ms over about
1,480 ms. At the head, 10 out of 10 passed, using 65 to 77 ms. Every run started at a one-minute
load from 15.67 to 23.45. Adding survivors did not force the cause. With 40 survivors, each read of
the group took longer to run too, so the share stayed under the bound, at 0.77.

**The change.** A round whose first read of states finds a process caught mid-exec cannot agree, since a round is kept only where neither of its state reads finds one. So once the census holds a round to keep, such a round stops at that first read. The census's pause is the base's, one look's pause per round, doubling from 1 ms to `LONGEST_PAUSE`. Where the time left would hold another round as long as the last, a pause is cut to leave it, so the census reads again before its deadline. That makes the pause shorter than the base's, never longer, and only where the base's pause would leave less than a round's length before the deadline. No test pins that cut: the census reads the clock itself, and the window is at most one pause, 50 ms, wide. In `:942`, every read of states shows the survivor mid-exec, so after its first whole round the census makes one read per pause, as it did before #553. A census whose rounds disagree in some other way still reads whole rounds, as at the base. Where the deadline passes first, it keeps the last whole round.

**What came before it, and why it went.** The first two heads took the other way out: each read of a round earned its own pause, and the census waited out a round's pauses together. That pause grows with the group, so a cap followed:
- The cap first left twice the last round's length. CI at `997947f` failed the 100-survivor test when the second round ran longer.
- The cap then left half of the time beyond one round. The reviewer on `e4db4b8` showed the cost. Under a forced slow second round (18 ms on each `ps` after the first 104), the base re-read and agreed, where the head ran out of time and kept the round that did not agree. That is the `D16` rule 3 harm the re-read item exists to prevent. Under rising load, the 100-survivor test failed for the same reason.
- The card's author revised item 7: after a disagreeing round, the head's pause is no longer than the base's.

Stopping a round that cannot agree meets that with the base's own pauses, and it removes the cause where it lies: in the reads a round makes, not in the pause after it.

**The new tests.**
- **The read-count test.** A census whose every read of states finds a process mid-exec makes a whole first round, then one read in each round after. The base makes five, whatever the load, so the test does not depend on the host's speed.
- **The 100-survivor test.** It kills 100 survivors whose first read of states shows each one mid-exec. It requires a second round that agrees within the deadline, and that begins with at least nine tenths of what the first round left; the base and the head both pause 1 ms there. With the earned pause and no cap, the census made one round and then waited out its deadline.

**This Linux cloud container.** Its PID 1, `process_api`, never reaps orphans, so a killed survivor
stays a zombie, and the reads after the kill poll until `UNREAPED_BOUND`. The `:942` test counts
those reads' processor time too: at `2dbc5e2`, unforced, it took 58 reads and 95.7 ms here, where
macOS CI took 36 reads. Under a subreaper that reaps at once, as macOS's launchd does, it took 30
to 42 reads. The 2 MiB pad also costs the caller about 5 ms a read here, more than a tenth of a
50 ms pause, so it failed the head too. A 512 KiB pad sits between the base's cost and the head's on
this host. Both were found in exploratory runs at `2dbc5e2`, where the base failed at 182 and 185
ms and the head passed at 102 to 113 ms. The counted runs are in #577.
