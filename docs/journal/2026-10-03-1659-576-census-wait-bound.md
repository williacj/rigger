# #576 — the exit census's wait had no read left after its kill

**What failed.** At `27be550` the exit cleanup's census of a dispatch's directory listed, killed and
waited within one read bound counted from its first read. The wait after the kill (`reaped`)
records a process as "still alive when L0's read bound ran out after its kill" only where one of its
own reads found it alive. Where the bound had already run out at its first read, that read was
refused as late, and the process went down as "not shown to have ended: the process table could
not be read". That string comes from one place only, `reaped`'s late branch with no earlier read,
so both failed runs went through it.

**Why the bound ran out.** Two full `lsof` listings of every process of the user take most of the
census's time. Traced with a probe build on 2026-10-03, at a load of about 8: 159 ms and 152 ms for
the two listings, 340 ms for the whole census before its wait, of the 1,000 ms bound. The failed
runs reached loads of 76 and 108, at which the census used up the bound before the wait's first
read. In the second failed run it ran out even earlier, before the census had named the process,
which is why that run recorded no name or command line.

**What changed.** The wait now reads within the cleanup's own bound counted from the census's first
kill, so it always gets reads of what the kill left, and it reads nothing past that bound of the
kill. A census that killed nothing still waits within its first bound.

**The forcing.** An `lsof` stand-in answers as `lsof` does until the census has sent the outside
process its kill. From then on it holds every listing until the bound gives it up. That uses up the
rest of the census's bound with no load, sleep or timer. At the base it gave the flake's reason in
10 of 10 runs; at the head it passed 10 of 10. The new test in `never-stop.test.mjs` holds the
listing the same way, and answers every read of the outside process before it at once, from what the
real tools printed before the cleanup. So no real listing before the kill can spend the bound and
send the census down its unnamed path, which a first version of the test allowed (Codex round 1).

**What it does not fix.** A census that cannot name the process within its bound still records it
unnamed. That was the second failed run, at a load of 108, and it needs the census's listings to fit
in 1,000 ms. It is #579's, not this card's.
