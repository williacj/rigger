## What was measured

Both probes ran at `origin/main` `4998448daf34ed545a803e557d08527d59ecc84e`, with `node` v26.13.0
on macOS 27.2 arm64, from throwaway scripts in the maker's scratch folder.

- **Today's behaviour.** Two events through `openSink`, then the unterminated bytes
  `{"ts":"1970-01-01T00:00:00.000Z","run":"r1","layer":"L5","event":"thr` appended directly. `readEvents`
  returned `one,two`. A second sink then emitted `four`, and `readEvents` threw
  `<dir>/events.jsonl line 3 is not a recorded event`, returning no event. Line 3 held the fragment
  with the whole `four` line run into it.
- **The separation.** `separate.mjs`, posted whole on the pull request, uses five sink-form lines.
  They hold nested objects whose first key is `ts`, a string holding `{"ts":"`, a backslash, a
  newline and a record-separator character (which `JSON.stringify` writes escaped), braces,
  non-ASCII text and deep nesting. Their byte prefixes, each line without its newline included,
  number 741. The leftmost offset from which the rest parsed as one JSON value was the whole line's
  start in every case:
  - each prefix glued onto a whole line: 741 of 741;
  - every ordered pair of prefixes glued onto a whole line: 549,081 of 549,081;
  - 20,000 seeded triples of prefixes glued onto a whole line: 20,000 of 20,000.

  No proper prefix parsed as one value (0 of 736), and no proper suffix of the six whole lines did
  (0 of 837). An earlier version of the script, posted first, wrote the record separator as a
  `\u001e` escape in its source, which the posted copy did not keep; its figures (740, 735, 836)
  are superseded by these.

## Why the reader, and not the writer

Every recovery the cards named breaks a ratified clause. A writer that terminates the fragment
with a newline needs E1's second rule changed anyway, and must first tell a fragment from another
process's line still being appended. At one read those are the same bytes, and telling them apart
needs a lock or state "Failure model" forbids. A writer that truncates can cut that line. So the
writers stay as they are, and L5's reader separates the fragment once a whole line follows it. The
probe above is the evidence that the separation has exactly one answer for the sink's line form.
