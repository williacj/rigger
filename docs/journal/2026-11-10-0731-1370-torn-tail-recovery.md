## What was measured

Both probes ran at `origin/main` `4998448daf34ed545a803e557d08527d59ecc84e`, with `node` v26.13.0
on macOS 27.2 arm64, from throwaway scripts in the maker's scratch folder.

- **Today's behaviour.** Two events through `openSink`, then the unterminated bytes
  `{"ts":"1970-01-01T00:00:00.000Z","run":"r1","layer":"L5","event":"thr` appended directly. `readEvents`
  returned `one,two`. A second sink then emitted `four`, and `readEvents` threw
  `<dir>/events.jsonl line 3 is not a recorded event`, returning no event. Line 3 held the fragment
  with the whole `four` line run into it.
- **The separation.** `separate.mjs`, posted whole on the pull request, uses six sink-form lines.
  They hold a string with a space in it (`reason: 'a b'`, the round-1 reviewer's case), leading and
  trailing spaces and a tab, nested objects whose first key is `ts`, and a string holding
  `{"ts":"`. They also hold a backslash, a newline and a record-separator character, which
  `JSON.stringify` writes escaped, plus braces, non-ASCII text and deep nesting. Their byte prefixes,
  each line without its newline included, number 894.
  - At any offset, a parser can start in more than one place: 13 of the 894 single-prefix cases
    parse from an earlier offset too. The reviewer's case parses from offsets 111 and 112, and the
    whole line starts at 112. JSON admits leading whitespace, so the first version of the delta's
    claim was false.
  - At offsets where the bytes `{"ts":"` begin, the rest parsed from the whole line's start and from
    no other in 894 of 894 single prefixes, 799,236 of 799,236 ordered pairs, and 20,000 of 20,000
    seeded triples.
  - No proper prefix parsed as one value (0 of 888), and no proper suffix of the seven whole lines
    did (0 of 997). No whole line holds a raw control character.

  Two earlier versions of the script are superseded. The first, posted in the PR's first comment,
  lost its record-separator escape when posted. The second, in the third comment, tried only offsets
  where `{` sits, which hid the whitespace case; the delta's sentence generalised past it.

## Why the reader, and not the writer

Every recovery the cards named breaks a ratified clause. A writer that terminates the fragment
with a newline needs E1's second rule changed anyway, and must first tell a fragment from another
process's line still being appended. At one read those are the same bytes, and telling them apart
needs a lock or state "Failure model" forbids. A writer that truncates can cut that line. So the
writers stay as they are, and L5's reader separates the fragment once a whole line follows it. The
probe above is the evidence that, at offsets where a sink line can begin, the separation has exactly
one answer.

Held events are the one place the sink itself changes. Their emits returned before naming, so
dropping them on a failed naming would break T1, as the round-1 review found. The sink now keeps an
event held until the append of its whole line returns, and appends what it holds ahead of each later
append. The hold stays in memory, as before naming, so "Failure model" is untouched. It cannot
outlive a sink whose every later append is refused: the end then writes those events to standard
error. Whether that path is outside `R-RECORD-6` is asked of the owner on #1370.
