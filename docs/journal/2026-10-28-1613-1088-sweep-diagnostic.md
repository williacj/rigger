The diagnostic looks only at numeric PIDs in completed `in use by pid` lines, after the sweep child returns. It performs read-only `ps` and `lsof` calls with a 5-second timeout on each command. It reports the executable name and only open paths or working directories physically under the fixture entry. It reports `disappeared`, `unanswered` or `could-not-inspect` where observation cannot establish a holder. It neither signals a PID nor changes the sweep's status or assertions. An `lsof` call by the sweep that never returns is outside this diagnostic boundary.

The three possible dispositions are a holder observed under the entry, a PID gone by follow-up, or a PID whose relationship to the entry remains unexplained. A hosted-Mac run can finish with no unexpected PID and an unknown historical cause.
