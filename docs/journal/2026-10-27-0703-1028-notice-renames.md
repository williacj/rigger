# The hold notice's renames live in L3

The architect's pre-dispatch attack on #1016, at acceptance revision `8571f543c0fa`, found that
`ARCHITECTURE.md` left two structures open for the notice. L2 could be handed a rename callback, or
L2 could answer each rename and let L3 make it. The attack ruled for the second. A closer found gone
is L3's fault to handle, and a callback would carry it through L2 untranslated, against boundary
rule 1. Answering a rename for L3 to act on is the shape `attempt` and the repo lane already take.
The owner ratified the drafted delta as drafted in
[U86](https://github.com/williacj/rigger/issues/740#issuecomment-6441360521). That included the
closing sentence on the closer's name as listed at a call's start, which the attack added after its
lint run, and which this card's lint run now covers.

The delta does four things:

1. Boundary rule 2 names what L2's notice function gives L3. Step 5 already handed L3 `<k>`,
   which the rule's old wording did not admit.
2. A paragraph after the hold's steps says L3 makes every rename between two of L2's answers.
3. The `-after-<k>` scan reads up to the newest number `<j>`, and passes over a number that names
   no issue. Stopping at the first such number misses a notice opened behind a pull request's
   number, and the next trigger then opens a second issue.
4. 100 lines move from the gate's row to L3's, so the package total stays 12,000.

The budget move is a judgement, not a measurement. By `node scripts/package-budget.mjs` at
`66e5b7c9`, L3's four files count 778 lines and the gate's one file 37. The attack judged #1016's
L3 share at 39 to 59 lines and M6-11c's at 20 to 40, so 800 could not hold both. It is written as
a move between rows, as #750's entry advises: a figure stated absolutely goes stale when another
card moves the same row. Where L3 counts under 850 once M6-11c merges, the difference goes back to
the gate. Where the gate passes 250, the move is reopened.
