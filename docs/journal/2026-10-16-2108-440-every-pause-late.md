# #440 — A loaded host ends pauses late, and the write kept room for 100 ms of it

## The cause

At base, `writeWhole` begins a pause only where it would end `SPARE` (100 ms) before its deadline.
That covers a final pause that ends up to 100 ms late, and nothing more. A loaded host can end a
pause later than that in two ways:

- **Every pause late.** A host loaded for the whole wait ends every pause late. The write had
  seen each earlier pause end late before it began the last, and still kept only 100 ms in hand.
- **Only the final pause late.** No pause before it runs late, so the write has nothing to see the
  stall coming by. It had waited until about 899 ms, so the final pause had 100 ms left to end in.

Two recorded failures ran past the bound under load, and neither was on this host:

- 1,058.5 ms at a one-minute load of 82, after PR #421;
- 1,002.97 ms in `:490`'s untouched case at a load of about 118, on PR #531.

Neither log holds a trace. So they cannot show which of the two ways happened. The cause here is
argued from the code and from forced traces, not from those logs.

## How the forcing showed it

Both forcings move the child's clock and change nothing else.

- **Every pause late:** each `Atomics.wait` ends 250 ms late. At base `cdbd247`, the never-touched
  end-only test (`:490`, S3) took 1,006.58 to 1,007.15 ms in 5 of 5 runs. Its fourth pause began
  at 755.43 ms, which the old rule allowed, and it ended past the bound.
- **Only the final pause late:** the final wait before the write gives up ends 250 ms late. The
  forcing asks the write's own give-up condition after each pause whether the write would give up
  1 ms on, and moves the clock only then. At base, S3 took 1,148.38 to 1,149.28 ms in 5 of 5 runs.
  The trace shows 737 pauses on time, the last beginning at 897.09 ms and alone ending late.

In every forced base trace, at most 0.83 ms of the measured time fell outside `writeWhole`. So
neither the fixture nor the test's own measure is where the time went.

## The fix

The write keeps two allowances in hand:

- the greater of `SPARE` and the latest any earlier pause of the same write ended past `PAUSE`;
- `WAIT`: it waits at most 600 ms on a pipe it found full. That leaves four tenths of the bound
  for a final pause to end late in.

At the head, on the main of `ba8ed63` with this change, the results were:

| Run | S3 took | Result |
|---|---|---|
| every pause late | 754.50 to 755.14 ms | 5 of 5 pass |
| only the final pause late | 850.45 to 851.32 ms | 5 of 5 pass |
| natural | 601.38 to 602.17 ms | 5 of 5 pass |

Under the final-pause forcing, the write began its last pause at 599.77 ms.

The 300 ms held-open test (S5) loses its first 300 ms before the pipe is found full. So `WAIT`
does not end its wait sooner, and it took 899.32 to 900.30 ms naturally. Its guards admit only the
200 ms between 800 and 1,000 ms.

## What stays open

`WAIT` shortens the longest a write waits on a reader that never drains, from about 899 ms to
600 ms. That is still over 500 times the 1.04 ms longest a draining reader was measured to leave
a pipe full.

A pause held longer than the bound has left still holds the write past it. The call cannot return
while its wait is blocked. Under the final-pause forcing, S5 and its touched twin took about
1,149 ms at the head. Their final pause begins at 899 ms, with 101 ms of the bound left. The card
puts that case outside its guarantee.
