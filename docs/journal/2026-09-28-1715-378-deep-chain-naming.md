ABOUTME: Journal for #378 (M2-03f): the kill now reads one process per level of a chain, and a
probe of how deep `main` names a chain exhausted the host's per-user process limit.

# #378: A deep chain is named as deep as the old main named it

## What was built

- After its first read of the whole group, a round of `killedOf` that has one survivor to look
  at reads that pid alone. Every round of a chain is such a round. Any other round reads the group
  again. The kill's reads add `pgid`, so a pid handed on to a process in another group is left out.
- A parent whose last child the read found a zombie is sent the kill on the strength of that
  row. The row names it as the parent, so it had not been reaped.
- `treeOf` keeps the survivors' tree as the kill ends it: the leaves, and the depth left for
  pacing. The old code passed over every process each round, and at 3,400 deep that
  bookkeeping cost 763 ms of 5,000. A scratch benchmark measured it with Node 26.5.0, 2026-09-28.

## What we learned

- **A read of several pids costs more than a read of the whole table.** Measured with `ps` from
  adv_cmds-240 on macOS 27.0 (26A428) on 2026-09-28, 200 reads of each form:

  | Form | Cost of one read |
  |---|---|
  | `-p` with one pid | 2.45 ms |
  | `-g` of a small group | 2.05 ms |
  | `-p` with two pids | 28.1 ms |
  | `-A` | 14.6 ms |

  My first cut read the survivor and its parent together. That put a 250-deep chain past the
  read timeout.
- **`main` at `6504435` is bounded by the process limit here, not by time.** On this host
  `kern.maxprocperuid` is 4,000. `main` named a chain 3,200 deep, and past about 3,300 its census
  could not start `ps` (`EAGAIN`).
- **What the probe cost other sessions.** It held about 3,200 perl processes at once, `fork`
  failed across the host, and other sessions' suites went red. So probe depth is bounded at the
  suite's 250.
- **Why items 1 and 2 went back to the author.** A measure at the edge cannot be taken on a
  shared host, so those items were returned on #378.
- **Two adapters in one process loop.** Each copy's `newListener` hook re-prepends its own signal
  listener ahead of the other's, until Node's async-hook map overflows. A measure must load one
  copy of the adapter per process.
