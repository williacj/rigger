other world-driven waits ran out before a world's first makers held, and the warm that fixes it.

# #593: a world's first maker paid the first-exec hold inside the settling wait

**What stayed false.** `settles` has two halves: every claim held at a started, unreleased
stand-in, and no pull trigger between its event and its claims. An instrumented copy of
`test/loop-world.mjs` printed both halves when the wait ran out. The trigger half held every
time. The claims half was false because no maker had written its held marker yet. With
`:87` run alone at a load of 21.03, those were cards 1 and 3, the world's first two makers.
L1 had recorded `dispatch.start` for both about 5,000 ms before the bound passed. In a
whole suite begun at a load of 12.18, the same thing happened in `loop.test.mjs:301` (cards 1,
2 and 3) and `redo-settle.test.mjs:83` (card 6).

**Why.** The time went in the exec itself, before the stand-in's first line. The instrumented
stand-in recorded its own process origin: 5,061 to 5,556 ms after `dispatch.start` in those
suite failures. Once running, each held within 1 ms. In 42 runs of `:87` alone at loads of
9.92 to 20.65, a world's first exec of its stand-in took 664 to 3,373 ms, and every later exec
took 20 to 63 ms. A standalone probe gave about 700 ms for the first exec of a freshly written
`#!node` script and 20 ms for its second. That is the hold `warmed` in
`test/process-fixtures.mjs` records: macOS assesses a file just written before its first exec
runs. Each world writes a fresh stand-in, so its first makers paid that hold inside the 5,000 ms
settling wait.

**Not #578.** The coordinator asked whether #578's change to the census bound caused this.
Twenty-four whole-file runs of `loop.test.mjs` and `redo-settle.test.mjs`, interleaved between
`605ca06` and `3d43dfb` at loads of 6.83 to 25.50, all passed at both refs. The diagnostic puts
the time before the stand-in runs, not in the census after it exits.

**The fix.** `heldStandIns` is built by every world before its loop. It now runs the stand-in
once with `NODE_OPTIONS=--import=data:text/javascript,process.exit()`. That exec pays the
assessment, and Node exits in the preload before the stand-in's code runs. So it records no
run, reads no plan and writes no marker. That matters because `test/loop-world.test.mjs` asserts
that a world's run record is empty. The wait and its bound are unchanged. In one whole suite at
the head, begun at a load of 19.11, the 134 warms took 83 to 192 ms each, median 96 ms.

**The forcing.** `c593-force.py` makes the stand-in's `claude` a shell. That shell holds a maker
run with `SIGSTOP` when the run is the file's first exec, until teardown kills it. A run handed
`--append-system-prompt-file` counts as a maker run, so the warm is exempt. The hold is a
stand-in for the system's assessment. In real life the warm pays that hold. In the forcing it is
waived, because no wait bounds the warm. With the forcing at the base, `:87` failed 10 of 10
with the original message. At the head it passed 10 of 10. With the warm removed under the
forcing, it failed 3 of 3.

**Reach.** Every world-driven wait on a held maker shares this cause. That includes the
`settling` and `positive` waits in `loop`, `loop-world`, `redo-settle` and `restart` that failed
on PR #592's pre-push runs. `report.test.mjs:239` builds its own `standInAgent` without a world,
so this warm does not reach it. #581 (PR #588) warms every stand-in helper, and it does reach
that test.

**Not `loop.test.mjs:952`.** That test, which timed out at 10,000 ms in #586's maker's runs, waits
for its makers with `until`, bounded by nothing but the test's own 10,000 ms. That bound covers
building the world as well, because the test builds its two worlds inside itself. So the warm
moves each world's first-exec hold out of the maker wait but leaves it inside the same bound.
The warm does not take :952's hold out of its bound.
