# #333 — deltas H1 to H5, with ruling 4's edits, placed in `ARCHITECTURE.md`

## What ran

The deltas come from three of the architect's rulings, all archived on #332:

- ruling 3 §7, for H1 and H5;
- ruling 2 §10, for H2 to H4;
- ruling 4 §7 and §6, for the edits H1-a, H1-b, H1-c, H2-a and H2-b, and for the Telemetry
  sentence H5 would otherwise contradict.

The owner ratified H1 to H5 at revision 5b, and accepted ruling 4 at "Wave-0 round 1". Each piece
was placed at the anchor its ruling names.

A throwaway script split every ratified block into sentences and compared each with the edited
file, whitespace-normalised. Every sentence appeared verbatim, apart from the three that ruling 4
replaces. Those three and every sentence the deltas replace were gone. Each of ruling 4's
insertions sat next to its anchor.

## What we learned

- Three anchors sit mid-paragraph: H2's two replacements, and H4 and H5 in Telemetry's first
  paragraph. Inserting there inline would have made each paragraph state several things, against
  `AGENTS.md`, "Write to be acted on". So the edit adds paragraph breaks at those anchors. Sentence
  order is kept, so the breaks are form, not meaning.
- The first round left three added lines past the file's 100-column prose wrap. It also left one
  line short where a sentence had been cut out of it. The second round rewrapped every paragraph
  the deltas touch to 100 columns, including boundary rule 2 whole, so the diff shows those
  paragraphs moving even where their words did not change. The verbatim check above covers them.
- Ruling 4 exists because the first round's text and a second card disagreed. The Telemetry
  sentence "The sink appends each event synchronously." contradicted H5, and the first round
  named the contradiction but did not settle it. Only a ruling could settle it, so the card went
  back to its author rather than the maker widening the diff.
